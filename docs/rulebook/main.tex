\chapter{The Shattered Reach}
For two centuries, the Meridian Gates bound the Reach into a single civilization. Then every gate fired at once. Worlds vanished behind walls of distorted space, fleets were cut in half, and the star maps became lies. The survivors remember that night simply as the Shattering.

Eighty-seven years later, the dead lanes flicker back to life, opening brief corridors to abandoned arsenals, isolated colonies, and systems unseen for generations. Whoever holds the anchor stations can decide which worlds reconnect---and on whose terms.

The Aurelian Compact claims a duty to restore the old union. The Veyr Dominion means to replace it with an order strong enough to survive the next collapse. The Kestrel Freeholds fight to keep the frontier unowned. Their wars are small by necessity: a handful of ships, minutes before a corridor closes, and entire systems in the balance.

\chapter{The battle at a glance}
In \game{}, two commanders guide small space fleets across a hex map. At the start of every turn, secretly allocate energy to movement, weapon charging, and shield reinforcement. The next twelve impulses reveal which speeds move, creating a tactical rhythm that rewards prediction over perfect calculation.
\section{What you need}
For tabletop play: a hex map no larger than 20 by 20, one die, markers for energy and damage, and ship sheets. The computer prototype supplies all of these automatically. For a browser skirmish, each player selects one to three ships; ship classes may be repeated. A solo opponent may match either the number of ships or their combined size value (small 2, medium 3, large 4).
\section{Victory}
Destroy enemy ships or score the most victory points from hull damage. A ship that leaves the map is destroyed.
\chapter{Turn sequence}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{Allocate energy secretly.} Engines generate energy every turn. Spend it on speed, powered weapons, and shield reinforcement. Unspent energy is lost.
\item \textbf{Announce speed and roll initiative.} The higher die chooses who resolves tied movement and firing first.
\item \textbf{Resolve twelve impulses.} Draw four cards from each of the three phase decks. A ship whose speed appears on the card moves one hex or turns sixty degrees.
\item \textbf{Fire weapons.} After movement, launch missiles, then resolve beams and mass drivers.
\item \textbf{End the turn.} Return allocated energy to undamaged engines, repair shields if funded, and begin again.
\end{enumerate}
\section{Movement}
A ship moves forward, turns only after satisfying its turn mode, or side-slips one hex without changing facing. Small, medium, and large ships have progressively stricter turn modes. Once per game, small and medium ships may use a special maneuver: Bootlegger (turn freely), Quick Stop, or Emergency Power (one extra move).

Beam and mass-driver weapons may only fire into their listed front, left, right, or aft arcs. Missiles use a full arc. Both beam and mass-driver weapons may target missiles, but suffer a $-1$ penalty on the die roll. A missile hit by either weapon is destroyed regardless of range.
\section{Shields and damage}
Front and aft shields absorb hits from their respective halves of the ship. Reinforcement absorbs damage for that turn. Damage past shields is rolled one point at a time: 1--3 damages hull, 4--5 damages an engine, and 6 destroys a weapon. A ship with no hull remaining is destroyed.

During energy allocation, a ship with a damaged shield may spend two energy to schedule one box of either its front or aft shield for repair. Restore that box at the end of the turn. A fully collapsed shield may be repaired; shield reinforcement is separate and cannot be assigned to a collapsed shield.
\chapter{Optional rules}
Select any combination of these rules before a battle begins. Both commanders use the same selected options for the entire engagement.

\section{Acceleration and deceleration limits}
Ships may set any speed on turn one. On later turns, a small ship may increase or decrease its previous speed by no more than 5, a medium ship by no more than 4, and a large ship by no more than 3. Engine damage that leaves a ship unable to sustain the minimum permitted speed forces it down to its maximum available speed.

\section{Weapon repair}
During energy allocation, a ship may spend three energy to repair one destroyed lance beam or mass driver. Missile launchers cannot be repaired. The repaired weapon returns immediately but remains offline for the rest of that turn; it may be powered normally beginning with the next turn.

\section{Fast turns}
When a ship turns 60 degrees after satisfying its turn mode, it also moves one hex forward in its new facing. That destination must be open. The forward movement counts as the first hex accumulated toward the ship's next turn.
\chapter{Fleets and tutorial}
\section{Fleet doctrine}
\textbf{Aurelian Compact} ships reward careful beam positioning. Heirs to the old gate authority, their commanders offer reunification at lance-point. \textbf{Veyr Dominion} fleets pressure opponents with mass drivers and missiles. The Dominion was forged by isolation and trusts centralized command above all else. \textbf{Kestrel Freeholds} combine durable hulls with flexible weapons. Their station clans and convoy captains fight to keep the reopened frontier unowned.

\section{Ships of the Reach}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=0pt]
\item[Aurelian Frigate] Swift Compact escorts race for the lane mouth, then rake intruders with disciplined crossing fire.
\item[Aurelian Cruiser] Compact cruisers broadcast terms of reunification while paired beam batteries make refusal expensive.
\item[Aurelian Battleship] A mobile fragment of the lost capital, built to make distant worlds remember Aurelian authority.
\item[Veyr Frigate] A patient hunter that marks targets for the Dominion and closes only when escape is impossible.
\item[Veyr Cruiser] The workhorse of Veyr blockade lines, equally prepared to hold a corridor or break one open.
\item[Veyr Battleship] The Dominion's answer to uncertainty: armored command, long-range fire, and no avenue of retreat.
\item[Kestrel Frigate] Courier frames turned raiders, fast enough to strike a claim beacon and vanish into the fracture lanes.
\item[Kestrel Cruiser] Freehold yards wrap stubborn shields around missile magazines built to outlast wealthier enemies.
\item[Kestrel Battleship] No two are identical; each is a shipyard covenant made armor, carrying a whole Freehold to war.
\end{description}
\section{First Light tutorial}
1. Allocate speed and one powered weapon. 2. Lock allocations and reveal initiative. 3. Draw an impulse card and observe movement. 4. Select a target in range, fire, and watch shields absorb the hit. The browser prototype guides these steps directly.
\section{Quick reference}
Speed maximum is 12. Missiles move at speed 24. Ships may not enter an occupied hex; missiles may. Each powered weapon fires once per turn. At the end of twelve impulses, reset for the next turn.
